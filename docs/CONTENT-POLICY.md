# Content policy

Wallweaver exists so people can make whatever they want into a wallpaper.
These rules keep the shared gallery legal and safe. They don't apply to
files you keep on your own computer.

## Your own creations come first

Anything you make is yours. You keep your copyright, you choose the license
in `wallpaper.json`, you keep all of your tips, and you can sell your work
anywhere you like.

## Fan art

Fan art of characters, games, shows and other franchises is welcome in the
gallery, under these conditions:

- It is tagged **Fan art**. Uploaders set the tag; moderators add it when it
  is missing.
- It is never sold through Wallweaver's premium packs.
- It is never used in Wallweaver's own promotion: no Featured or Staff pick
  placement, no official videos, screenshots, mockups or store pages.
- Rights holders can have it taken down through the process below, and we
  honor those requests.

The project itself only uses other people's brands, characters or logos
through licensed **Official collabs**, which carry a verified badge.

## Not allowed

- Malware, crypto miners, trackers, or hidden network calls. Every network
  destination must be declared in `wallpaper.json`; a package that contacts
  anything else is removed and its signing key revoked.
- Executables, native plugins, or attempts to get around the sandbox.
- Re-uploads of someone else's work without their permission, including
  removing or changing credit on a remix.
- Sexual content involving minors, real people's intimate images, content
  that promotes violence against people, and anything illegal where the
  gallery is hosted. Mature content, when the gallery supports it, will be
  hidden by default and never featured.
- AI-generated wallpapers that aren't labeled as AI-generated.

## Takedowns

Anyone can report a gallery entry with the in-app **Report** button or by
opening an issue with the report template. For copyright and trademark
claims we need:

1. the gallery entry or package id
2. what work or mark you hold rights to, and proof or a public link
3. a way to reach you, and a statement that the claim is accurate

What happens next is public: the request and the outcome are logged in the
gallery repository, with personal details removed. Valid claims are removed
from the index and the package is added to the revocation list, which
clients check daily; a revoked package is disabled and the user is told why.
Uploaders can respond if they believe a takedown is mistaken.

Gallery index entries may point at files hosted by the creator. That way a
takedown removes one entry, and one dispute can't take down the whole
gallery repository.

## Repeat infringers

An uploader with three upheld takedowns within twelve months loses gallery
upload access, and their signing keys are no longer accepted for new
submissions. Severe cases (malware, deliberate impersonation) lose access on
the first offense.

## Changes

This policy will change as the gallery grows. Changes are made by pull
request so the history is public.
